# ActOS — Vision

> **Document role:** the anchor. Defines what we're building and why. Changes rarely.
> **Read alongside:** `01-AUDIENCE.md` for context on who this is for.

---

## One-liner

**Decide what you're doing today. For your goals.**

ActOS is a daily-planning tool for ambitious people working toward big goals — the kind that take months or years and require action *every day*, not "sometime next week."

> *You solve sadness with knowledge, and anxiety with action.*

---

## What we are, and what we're against

**ActOS is anti-tasks, not anti-work.**

We're not building a tool for people who want to do less. We're building a tool for people who already work hard and want their hard work to land — to add up to something real, instead of dissipating across task lists, calendar slots, and "system" tweaks that feel like progress but aren't.

The competition isn't laziness. It's **busyness** — time spent in productivity apps that simulate progress without producing it. Less busyness, more business.

Ambitious people don't lack drive. They have plenty. What they lack is structure that channels their drive toward what they actually committed to. ActOS is that structure.

---

## Core Insight

Most productivity tools let you defer. Schedule something for Friday and forget about it. Plan a week and feel productive without doing anything.

But for **big goals** — launching a business, getting in shape, building a skill, finishing a creative work — deferral is the enemy. Progress comes from doing *something* today. Every day. Not from rearranging future calendar slots.

ActOS is built around a single daily question: **what are you doing today for your goals?** Not next week. Not on Tuesday. Today.

The product enforces this through structure:
- You set 2–3 active goals.
- You break them into closeable projects.
- You decompose projects into concrete actions ≤ 2 hours each.
- Every morning, you choose what you're doing today from those actions.
- Every evening, you see what was done.
- The metrics show you honestly: what you actually moved forward, vs how much you spent doing it.

## Three Foundational Principles

### 1. Today is the only horizon

You don't schedule actions for next Wednesday. You don't put things in calendar slots. The question ActOS asks every morning is: **of all the actions in your goals' projects, which ones are you doing today?**

This is harder than scheduling. It forces honest weekly cadence — if a project hasn't seen action in two weeks, you see it. If a goal is going untouched, you see it. There's no "I scheduled it for Friday so it counts."

The only thing scheduled is today.

### 2. Value ≠ Effort

Progress toward a goal is not the same as personal workload. ActOS tracks both, separately:

- **Value**: did the thing get done? (by you OR via delegation, both contribute)
- **Effort**: how much of your personal capacity did it cost?

A delegated action moves the goal forward at full Value but costs only 20% Effort. Doing it yourself costs 100% Effort. This honest separation removes guilt around delegation and gives a real picture of progress vs personal load.

The same 20% discount applies to Time Invested for delegated actions — symmetric and honest.

### 3. Closeable projects

A project is something that can be **completed within a foreseeable horizon** — not "launch a YouTube channel" (a goal), but "shoot video #1" (a closeable unit). Each closed project is a visible tick of progress toward the goal.

If a project doesn't close for months, it's poorly decomposed. ActOS surfaces this; the user decides what to do.

## Philosophy

### ActOS is a tool, not a coach

The system gives structure and visibility. It does not push, judge, evaluate, or gamify in infantilizing ways. A craftsman picks up the tool when they need it; the tool doesn't nag.

This means:
- No notifications shaming inactivity.
- No "you missed 3 days" guilt screens.
- No badges, achievements, or streaks-as-pressure.
- No suggestions like "you should delegate this."

What remains is honest, visible progress — which is the actual cure for goal-anxiety.

### Built-in discipline of decomposition

ActOS quietly teaches the right way to break work down:
- **Few goals** (max 2–3 active) → forced focus.
- **Closeable projects** → forced right-sizing.
- **Actions ≤ 2 hours** → forced concreteness.
- **Daily plan, not weekly schedule** → forced cadence.
- **Progress = closed projects + ritual consistency** → motivates correct decomposition.

This isn't a feature. It's the DNA of the system. Other tools give empty fields; ActOS shapes how you fill them.

### Execution layer: Sessions

For the moments when you need to actually *do* the work — not just see it on a list — ActOS includes Focus Sessions. Pick what you'll work on, set a timer, optionally take breaks, and execute. The session records what you finished, how long you focused, and what value you added.

Sessions are not a habit-tracking gimmick. They're a serious execution mode for the moments when you need to commit.

### Reality of growing projects

Projects grow, especially when the user is doing something for the first time. They underestimate. They miss steps. This is normal and valuable — it's how learning happens.

ActOS does not punish project growth. It surfaces it visibly ("this project has grown from 4 to 12 actions over 14 days") and lets the user decide: split it, close-and-continue, or accept and proceed.

---

## What ActOS is **not**

- Not a calendar / scheduling tool ("plan my Friday meetings"). The only horizon is today.
- Not a journaling-first product (reflections are part of daily close, not the core).
- Not a habit tracker (rituals are one mode of action, not the core).
- Not a team collaboration tool (single-user system, at least in v1).
- Not a coach, AI assistant, or therapy app.
- Not a multi-mode toggle-everything-you-want app. The daily ritual is the product.

ActOS does not try to replace any of these. It occupies a different slot: the bridge between high-level ambition and daily execution, with built-in decomposition discipline and the discipline of "what are you doing today?" rather than "what's on Friday?"

---

## Why this matters

The user we're building for is not lazy and not undisciplined. They're **overwhelmed by possibility**. Every day brings new options, new ideas, new "should I be doing this instead?" anxiety.

The cure is not more options or smarter prioritization. The cure is **clarity of motion**: I have 2–3 goals, I know what's moving me toward them today, I see what I'm spending time on, and I see I'm progressing.

Daily, not weekly. Concrete, not deferred. Visible, not hidden in a calendar.

ActOS provides that clarity. The anxiety relief is a side effect of well-designed structure, not a marketed feature.

You solve sadness with knowledge — by understanding what's true. You solve anxiety with action — by doing the next concrete thing. ActOS is built around the second half.
