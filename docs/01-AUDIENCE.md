# ActOS — Audience

> **Document role:** defines who we build for. Used to validate every product decision: "does this serve our user?"
> **Read alongside:** `00-VISION.md`.

---

## The User in One Sentence

An ambitious person with multiple directions of growth, who loses focus and feels anxious because there are too many possibilities and not enough clarity about what's actually moving them forward.

---

## Profile

**Mindset.** Driven, intelligent, self-aware. Reads productivity content. Has tried multiple tools. Genuinely wants to build, achieve, grow — across several life areas at once.

**Life context.** Pursuing 2–4 meaningful directions simultaneously. Examples:
- Lose weight, get stronger
- Launch a business
- Learn a new language
- Start a YouTube channel / blog / podcast
- Master a new skill (design, code, music, writing)
- Read more, travel, build relationships

These rarely come one at a time. Modern ambitious people pursue several in parallel, which is exactly the source of the problem.

**Tooling.** Likely already using some combination of: Notion, Todoist, Sunsama, Apple Notes, Google Calendar, paper journals, habit trackers. None of them have stuck for the deep need.

**Demographics.** Broadly, 25–45. Knowledge worker, founder, freelancer, creator, or ambitious employee. Tech-comfortable but not necessarily a power user.

---

## The Real Pain

It is not "I don't have a task list."

It is:

> *"I have too many possibilities, I feel I'm always missing something, and I can't tell if I'm actually moving forward in any direction that matters."*

This is **possibility-overwhelm and progress-anxiety**, not lack of organization. Most existing tools solve organization. They do not solve this.

The pain has four parts:

### 1. Decision fatigue
Every day brings new options. Every week brings a new "shiny thing" — a course, an opportunity, a side project. The user is exhausted from constantly re-evaluating priorities.

### 2. FOMO across goals
Working on goal A means *not* working on goals B, C, D. The user feels guilty about whichever one they're not currently working on.

### 3. Invisible progress
Even when working hard, the user can't tell if they're meaningfully closer to anything. Days fill with activity, but the question "did I actually move forward?" stays unanswered.

### 4. Scheduling as deferral
Tools that let you schedule for "Friday" or "next week" feel productive but enable deferral. The user adds a task to Friday's calendar, feels resolved, and the goal stays untouched. Big goals don't move on Friday's plan — they move on today's action. The user needs a tool that asks "what are you doing TODAY?" not "what's on your calendar?"

---

## What the user is actually looking for

In their own words, roughly:

- *"I want to commit to 2–3 things and stop second-guessing."*
- *"I want to know what I'm doing today for them — not next Wednesday."*
- *"I want to see clearly what moves me toward what I committed to."*
- *"I want to know it counts when I delegate, not just when I personally execute."*
- *"I want to see I'm making progress, even on weeks that felt scattered."*
- *"I want a tool that respects my intelligence and doesn't nag me."*

---

## Use cases (what triggers them to use ActOS)

- Starting a new chapter (new job, new business, new health journey).
- After burnout or burnout-adjacent state — wanting to rebuild more sustainably.
- When existing tools (Notion, Todoist, paper) have failed for the third time.
- When realizing they've been "busy but not progressing" for months.
- Beginning of a year / quarter, with new goals to commit to.

---

## Who is **not** the audience

- People who want one simple to-do list. (Use Apple Reminders.)
- People with no goals beyond daily survival. (ActOS is for ambition.)
- Teams looking to collaborate. (ActOS is single-user, at least v1.)
- People wanting a dopamine-driven gamified experience. (ActOS is sober.)
- People wanting an AI life coach to tell them what to do. (ActOS is a tool, not a coach.)
- Hardcore productivity nerds optimizing 5%. (ActOS is for ambition, not optimization.)
- People who want a calendar / scheduling app. (Use Sunsama, Fantastical. ActOS doesn't schedule; it asks "what are you doing today?")

## What this means for product decisions

Every product decision is validated against this user. Some implications:

- **Onboarding asks for commitment** (set a goal, define a project, create actions) — because this user respects a serious tool and dislikes empty shells.
- **Daily plan is the central ritual** — every morning the user picks today's actions. The tool doesn't let them schedule for Friday and forget; the only horizon is today.
- **No motivational copy** — this user finds it patronizing.
- **No streaks-as-pressure, no shame mechanics** — this user is already too hard on themselves.
- **Visible progress is non-negotiable** — without it, the product fails its core promise.
- **Delegation is first-class** — this user often has resources (money, AI, helpers) but feels guilty using them.
- **Decomposition guidance is gentle, not enforced** — this user wants help, not parenting.
